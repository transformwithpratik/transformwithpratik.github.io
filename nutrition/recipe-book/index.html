Moong dal chilla with paneer filling

Two crisp lentil pancakes stuffed with spiced paneer. A filling breakfast with over 20 g of protein and no protein powder.

403 kcal · 25 g protein · 41 g carbs · 16 g fat · serves 1 · 25 min

Ingredients

  • 60 g yellow moong dal, soaked 3 to 4 hours
  • 50 g paneer, crumbled
  • 1 small onion, finely chopped
  • ½ tomato, finely chopped
  • 1 tsp oil
  • Green chilli, ginger, coriander, salt, ¼ tsp turmeric

Method

  1. Drain the soaked dal and blend with the chilli, ginger, salt and a few tablespoons of water into a smooth, pourable batter.
  2. Mix the paneer with half the onion, the tomato and coriander for the filling.
  3. Heat a non-stick tawa, brush with a few drops of oil and spread half the batter thin. Sprinkle the remaining onion on top.
  4. Cook on medium heat until the edges lift, flip, and cook 1 minute more.
  5. Spread half the filling on one side, fold, and repeat for the second chilla.

Tip. Soak the dal the night before so breakfast takes 15 minutes. Batter keeps 2 days in the fridge.

Besan and spinach chilla with curd dip

A 15-minute savoury pancake made from chickpea flour and spinach, served with a thick hung-curd dip.

328 kcal · 23 g protein · 36 g carbs · 10 g fat · serves 1 · 15 min

Ingredients

  • 50 g besan (about 5 tbsp)
  • A handful of spinach, chopped
  • 2 tbsp chopped onion
  • 1 tsp oil
  • 100 g hung curd, for the dip
  • Ajwain, chilli, salt, mint for the dip

Method

  1. Whisk the besan with salt, ajwain and about 80 ml water into a lump-free batter. Stir in the spinach and onion.
  2. Heat a tawa, add a few drops of oil and spread the batter into one large or two small chillas.
  3. Cook 2 to 3 minutes per side until golden spots appear.
  4. Mix the hung curd with chopped mint, salt and a pinch of roasted jeera for the dip.

Tip. Pack the chilla in a foil roll with the dip in a small box for an office breakfast.

Overnight oats with whey and chia

Mix it in two minutes at night, eat it cold the next morning. About 37 g of protein for a commute breakfast.

499 kcal · 37 g protein · 61 g carbs · 13 g fat · serves 1 · 5 min

Ingredients

  • 50 g rolled oats
  • 150 ml toned milk
  • 1 scoop (30 g) whey protein
  • 2 tsp chia seeds
  • ½ banana, sliced

Method

  1. In a jar, whisk the whey into the milk first so it does not clump.
  2. Stir in the oats and chia seeds. Close the jar and refrigerate overnight.
  3. In the morning, top with banana. Loosen with a splash of milk if it is too thick.

Tip. No whey? Use 150 g hung curd instead of the whey and half the milk.

Soya chunk pulao with raita

A one-pot lunch where soya chunks do the heavy lifting on protein. Packs well for the office.

528 kcal · 31 g protein · 75 g carbs · 11 g fat · serves 1 · 35 min

Ingredients

  • 40 g soya chunks (dry weight)
  • 60 g rice, rinsed and soaked 15 minutes
  • ¼ cup green peas
  • 1 small onion, sliced
  • 1½ tsp oil or ghee
  • 100 g curd, for raita
  • Bay leaf, jeera, ginger-garlic paste, garam masala, salt

Method

  1. Soak the soya chunks in hot salted water for 10 minutes, then squeeze out all the water.
  2. Heat the oil in a pressure cooker, add jeera and bay leaf, then the onion. Cook until golden.
  3. Add ginger-garlic paste, the soya chunks, peas and garam masala. Stir for 2 minutes.
  4. Add the drained rice and 1¼ cups water with salt. Pressure cook for 2 whistles and rest 10 minutes.
  5. Whisk the curd with salt and roasted jeera and serve on the side.

Tip. Squeezing the soaked chunks well is what makes them soak up the masala instead of tasting rubbery.

Paneer bhurji

Scrambled paneer with onion, tomato and capsicum. Ten minutes, one pan, about 20 g of protein.

342 kcal · 20 g protein · 9 g carbs · 26 g fat · serves 1 · 15 min

Ingredients

  • 100 g paneer, crumbled
  • 1 small onion, chopped
  • 1 tomato, chopped
  • ¼ capsicum, chopped
  • 1 tsp oil
  • Jeera, green chilli, turmeric, chilli powder, salt, coriander

Method

  1. Heat the oil, add jeera and chilli, then the onion. Cook until soft.
  2. Add the capsicum and tomato with turmeric, chilli powder and salt. Cook until the tomato breaks down.
  3. Add the paneer, mix gently and cook 2 minutes. Finish with coriander.

Tip. For fat loss, swap half the paneer for 50 g crumbled firm tofu. The texture is almost identical.

Rajma brown rice bowl

Classic rajma with brown rice and a crunchy cucumber salad. Plenty of fibre to keep you full through the afternoon.

439 kcal · 17 g protein · 79 g carbs · 7 g fat · serves 1 · 50 min

Ingredients

  • 50 g rajma, soaked overnight
  • 50 g brown rice
  • 1 small onion, chopped
  • 1 large tomato, pureed
  • 1 tsp oil
  • ½ cucumber, diced, for salad
  • Ginger-garlic paste, rajma masala or garam masala, salt

Method

  1. Pressure cook the soaked rajma with salt for 5 to 6 whistles until fully soft.
  2. Cook the brown rice with twice its volume of water for about 25 minutes, or 3 whistles in a cooker.
  3. Heat the oil, cook the onion until brown, add ginger-garlic paste, then the tomato and masala. Cook until oil separates.
  4. Add the rajma with its water and simmer 10 minutes, mashing a few beans to thicken.
  5. Serve over the rice with the cucumber salad on the side.

Tip. Cook rajma in bulk on Sunday. It keeps 4 days in the fridge and freezes well.

Chana chaat

Tangy chickpea salad you can assemble at your desk. No cooking if you keep boiled chana in the fridge.

275 kcal · 14 g protein · 48 g carbs · 4 g fat · serves 1 · 10 min

Ingredients

  • 1 cup boiled kabuli chana (150 g)
  • ¼ onion, chopped
  • ½ tomato, chopped
  • ¼ cucumber, chopped
  • Juice of ½ lemon
  • Chaat masala, roasted jeera, green chilli, coriander, salt

Method

  1. Combine the chana, onion, tomato and cucumber in a bowl.
  2. Add lemon juice, chaat masala, roasted jeera, chilli and salt. Toss.
  3. Top with coriander and eat within a few hours so the vegetables stay crisp.

Tip. Add 50 g crumbled paneer for about 9 g more protein.

Moong sprouts salad with peanuts

Home-sprouted moong with pomegranate, cucumber and a few peanuts for crunch. A light snack that travels well.

245 kcal · 14 g protein · 38 g carbs · 6 g fat · serves 1 · 13 min

Ingredients

  • 40 g whole moong (dry weight), sprouted
  • 2 tbsp chopped onion
  • ½ tomato, chopped
  • ¼ cucumber, chopped
  • 2 tbsp pomegranate seeds
  • 1 tbsp roasted peanuts
  • Juice of ½ lemon
  • Chaat masala, salt, coriander

Method

  1. Soak the moong for 8 hours, drain, and leave covered for 12 to 24 hours until short sprouts appear.
  2. Steam or boil the sprouts for 2 to 3 minutes. This makes them easier to digest. Cool.
  3. Toss with the vegetables, pomegranate, lemon juice, chaat masala and salt. Top with peanuts.

Tip. Macros are based on the dry weight of moong before sprouting, which is the easiest way to measure.

Tofu tikka

Firm tofu marinated in hung curd and spices, cooked in a pan or air fryer with capsicum and onion.

323 kcal · 32 g protein · 12 g carbs · 19 g fat · serves 1 · 32 min

Ingredients

  • 150 g firm tofu, pressed and cubed
  • 50 g hung curd
  • ½ capsicum, in squares
  • 1 small onion, in petals
  • 1 tsp oil
  • Ginger-garlic paste, kashmiri chilli, tandoori masala, kasuri methi, lemon, salt

Method

  1. Press the tofu between kitchen towels under a heavy pan for 10 minutes, then cube.
  2. Mix the hung curd with ginger-garlic paste, spices, kasuri methi, lemon and salt. Coat the tofu and vegetables. Rest 15 minutes.
  3. Cook in a hot pan with the oil for 8 to 10 minutes, turning to char each side. Or air fry at 200°C for 12 minutes.

Tip. Use paneer instead of tofu for a richer version; the calories go up by about 180.

Hung curd parfait

Layers of thick curd, apple, oats and almonds. A sweet-tooth snack with 20+ g of protein.

343 kcal · 25 g protein · 40 g carbs · 10 g fat · serves 1 · 5 min

Ingredients

  • 200 g hung curd or plain Greek yogurt
  • ½ apple, diced
  • 2 tbsp oats, dry-roasted
  • 7 to 8 almonds, chopped
  • 1 tsp honey
  • Cinnamon

Method

  1. Dry-roast the oats in a pan for 2 minutes until fragrant.
  2. In a glass or box, layer half the curd, half the apple and oats, then repeat.
  3. Top with almonds, cinnamon and honey.

Tip. To make hung curd, tie regular curd in a muslin cloth and hang it in the fridge for 4 to 6 hours.

Namkeen sattu drink

The Bihari classic: roasted chana flour whisked with water, lemon and jeera. Ready in 2 minutes before a workout.

158 kcal · 9 g protein · 26 g carbs · 2 g fat · serves 1 · 2 min

Ingredients

  • 40 g sattu (4 tbsp)
  • Juice of ½ lemon
  • 1 tbsp finely chopped onion (optional)
  • Roasted jeera powder, black salt, mint, 300 ml cold water

Method

  1. Whisk the sattu into the water until smooth.
  2. Add lemon, roasted jeera, black salt, mint and onion. Stir and drink.

Tip. Sattu values vary by brand; check the pack for protein per 100 g.

Masoor dal palak with two rotis

Everyday dal made heartier with spinach, served with two rotis and a spoon of ghee. A complete plate.

458 kcal · 22 g protein · 81 g carbs · 8 g fat · serves 1 · 30 min

Ingredients

  • 50 g masoor dal
  • A large handful of spinach
  • ½ tomato
  • ¼ onion
  • 60 g atta, for 2 rotis
  • 1 tsp ghee, for tadka
  • Jeera, garlic, red chilli, turmeric, salt

Method

  1. Pressure cook the dal with turmeric, salt and 1½ cups water for 2 whistles.
  2. Add chopped spinach and tomato and simmer 5 minutes.
  3. For the tadka, heat ghee, add jeera, garlic, onion and red chilli, and pour over the dal.
  4. Make two thin rotis from the atta and serve.

Tip. A katori of curd (150 g) on the side adds about 5 g of protein.

Egg bhurji on whole wheat toast

Two whole eggs plus two whites, scrambled desi-style and served on toast. For eggetarians who want close to 28 g of protein, fast.

382 kcal · 28 g protein · 29 g carbs · 17 g fat · serves 1 · 13 min

Ingredients

  • 2 whole eggs
  • 2 egg whites
  • ¼ onion, chopped
  • ½ tomato, chopped
  • 1 tsp oil
  • 2 slices whole wheat bread
  • Green chilli, turmeric, pepper, salt, coriander

Method

  1. Whisk the eggs and whites with salt and turmeric.
  2. Cook the onion, chilli and tomato in the oil for 3 minutes.
  3. Pour in the eggs and stir on low heat until just set.
  4. Serve on toasted bread with coriander and pepper.

Tip. Cooking on low heat keeps the eggs soft; high heat makes them dry and rubbery.

Roasted makhana and peanuts

A crunchy desk-drawer snack that beats namkeen. Make a week's worth in 10 minutes.

220 kcal · 9 g protein · 22 g carbs · 12 g fat · serves 1 · 10 min

Ingredients

  • 30 g makhana (about 1½ cups)
  • 1 tbsp peanuts
  • ¾ tsp ghee
  • Black pepper, chaat masala or peri-peri, salt

Method

  1. Dry-roast the peanuts in a pan for 3 minutes and set aside.
  2. Warm the ghee, add makhana and roast on low heat for 5 to 6 minutes, stirring, until they crunch when pressed.
  3. Toss with peanuts and seasoning. Cool fully before storing in an airtight jar.

Tip. This is a lighter, lower-protein snack. Pair it with a glass of milk or a protein shake if you need more.

Paneer and veg wrap

A whole wheat roti rolled around spiced paneer, crunchy vegetables and a mint-curd sauce. Easy to eat at your desk.

443 kcal · 24 g protein · 44 g carbs · 21 g fat · serves 1 · 20 min

Ingredients

  • 50 g atta, for one large roti
  • 75 g paneer, in strips
  • ¼ onion, sliced
  • ¼ capsicum, sliced
  • A handful of shredded cabbage
  • 2 tbsp hung curd with mint
  • ½ tsp oil
  • Tikka masala, chaat masala, salt

Method

  1. Make one large, thin roti.
  2. Toss the paneer, onion and capsicum with masala and cook in the oil for 4 minutes.
  3. Spread the mint-curd on the roti, add cabbage and the paneer mix, and roll tightly.
  4. Wrap in butter paper or foil to carry.

Tip. Use a ready whole wheat wrap if you are short on time; check the label for calories.

Palak tofu

Palak paneer's lighter cousin: tofu in a smooth spinach gravy. Fewer calories, similar protein.

336 kcal · 31 g protein · 15 g carbs · 21 g fat · serves 1 · 30 min

Ingredients

  • 150 g firm tofu, cubed
  • 1 large bunch spinach (150 g)
  • 1 small onion
  • ½ tomato
  • 1½ tsp oil
  • Garlic, ginger, green chilli, jeera, garam masala, salt

Method

  1. Blanch the spinach in boiling water for 2 minutes, then plunge into cold water and blend smooth with chilli.
  2. Heat the oil, add jeera, garlic, ginger and onion; cook until soft. Add tomato and cook down.
  3. Add the spinach puree, salt and garam masala, and simmer 5 minutes.
  4. Add the tofu and simmer 3 more minutes.

Tip. Serve with one roti or a small bowl of rice depending on your carb target.

Moong dal vegetable khichdi

Comfort food that still fits a plan. Moong dal, rice and vegetables cooked together, with curd on the side.

436 kcal · 19 g protein · 70 g carbs · 9 g fat · serves 1 · 30 min

Ingredients

  • 40 g yellow moong dal
  • 40 g rice
  • ¼ carrot, diced
  • 2 tbsp peas
  • 4 to 5 french beans, chopped
  • 1 tsp ghee
  • 100 g curd, on the side
  • Jeera, hing, turmeric, ginger, salt

Method

  1. Rinse the dal and rice together.
  2. Heat ghee in a cooker, add jeera, hing and ginger, then the vegetables. Stir 2 minutes.
  3. Add dal, rice, turmeric, salt and 3 cups water. Pressure cook for 3 whistles.
  4. Rest, stir well, and serve with curd.

Tip. Use more dal than rice (for example 50 g dal and 30 g rice) to push protein higher.

Poha with peanuts and peas, curd on the side

A lighter Maharashtrian-style poha. Poha alone is low in protein, so this one comes with a bowl of curd.

431 kcal · 14 g protein · 56 g carbs · 18 g fat · serves 1 · 15 min

Ingredients

  • 50 g thick poha
  • 1 tbsp peanuts
  • 2 tbsp peas
  • ¼ onion, chopped
  • 1 tsp oil
  • A squeeze of lemon
  • 150 g curd, on the side
  • Mustard seeds, curry leaves, turmeric, green chilli, salt, coriander

Method

  1. Rinse the poha in a strainer, drain and leave for 5 minutes to soften.
  2. Heat oil, roast the peanuts, then add mustard seeds, curry leaves and chilli.
  3. Add the onion and peas and cook 3 minutes. Add turmeric, salt and the poha. Mix gently on low heat for 2 minutes.
  4. Finish with lemon and coriander and serve with curd.

Tip. Swap the curd for 150 g hung curd to add about 10 g more protein.
