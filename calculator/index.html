How these numbers are calculated

Calories. Your resting energy use (BMR) comes from the Mifflin-St Jeor equation, which is the most accurate of the common formulas for adults in validation studies. It is multiplied by an activity factor to estimate maintenance calories (TDEE). Fat loss uses a 20 percent deficit (10 percent for "slowly"); muscle gain uses a 10 percent surplus. Fat-loss targets are never set below your BMR, 1,500 kcal for men or 1,200 kcal for women.

Protein. 1.6 to 2.2 g per kg of body weight per day covers what research shows for people who strength train. We use the top of that range during fat loss to help keep muscle. If your BMI is above 25, the calculation uses the weight you would have at a BMI of 25, so targets stay realistic.

Fat and carbs. Fat is set at about 25 percent of calories (never below 0.6 g per kg). Carbohydrates fill the rest.

Expected change. We use roughly 7,700 kcal per kg of body weight. Real progress is not a straight line: water, salt and hormones move the scale day to day, so judge the trend over two to three weeks.

These are starting points, not prescriptions. Track your weight and adjust by 100 to 200 kcal if the trend does not match your goal after a few weeks.
