Eating-out guide

2 min read · By Pratik Jain

Team lunches, client dinners and weekend plans are part of life. You do not have to skip them or order a sad salad. A few defaults go a long way.

Three rules that work anywhere

  1. Decide before you arrive. Look at the menu online and pick your order while you are not hungry.
  2. Protein first. Build the meal around a protein dish, then add one carb.
  3. Ask for it lighter. "Less butter", "less oil" and "gravy on the side" are normal requests in most Indian restaurants.

North Indian

  • Good picks: paneer or soya tikka, tandoori mushrooms, dal tadka, chana masala, mixed vegetables, raita, salad.
  • Breads: tandoori roti over butter naan or kulcha.
  • Go easy on: dal makhani, malai kofta, paneer butter masala and other cream-heavy gravies. Share one rather than having your own.

South Indian

  • Good picks: idli with sambar, pesarattu (moong dal dosa), plain or onion uttapam, a bowl of curd.
  • Ask for an extra bowl of sambar; it is one of the easier ways to add protein here.
  • Go easy on: vada, ghee roast dosa and coconut chutney refills.

Indo-Chinese

  • Good picks: clear or hot-and-sour soup, chilli paneer or tofu dry, steamed momos, stir-fried vegetables.
  • Go easy on: deep-fried starters, gravy versions with thick sauces, fried rice and noodles as a whole plate.

Cafes

  • Good picks: paneer or egg sandwiches on whole wheat, omelettes, Greek yogurt bowls, salads with paneer or chickpeas.
  • Coffee: a regular latte is fine. Flavoured, whipped and frappe drinks can carry as much sugar as dessert.

Drinks and dessert

  • Alcohol adds calories and tends to loosen food choices. If you drink, alternate with water and decide your limit in advance.
  • Sweetened lassi, mocktails and cold drinks add up fast. Chaas, lime soda without sugar or water are easy swaps.
  • Want dessert? Share it. A few spoons usually satisfy the craving.

The next day

No compensating with skipped meals or punishment workouts. Go back to your normal plan, drink water, get your steps in. One meal out does not change your weekly trend.

General information, not medical advice. If you have a health condition, are pregnant or take medication, check with your doctor before changing your diet, training or supplements.
