Nutrition fundamentals

3 min read · By Pratik Jain

You do not need a perfect diet. You need a few numbers that point in the right direction and meals you can repeat on a busy day. Here is the short version of what matters, in order.

1. Calories decide the direction

Your weight trend follows your average energy balance over weeks, not any single meal. Eat a little less than you burn and you lose weight; a little more and you gain. The calculator gives you a starting target.

  • For fat loss, a moderate deficit (10 to 20 percent below maintenance) is easier to keep up and helps protect muscle.
  • Losing about 0.5 to 1 percent of your body weight per week is a pace most people can sustain while keeping strength.
  • For muscle gain, a small surplus (around 10 percent) is enough. More mostly adds fat.

2. Protein protects and builds muscle

If you strength train, research supports about 1.6 g of protein per kg of body weight per day, with benefits possibly continuing up to about 2.2 g per kg. Spreading it over 3 or 4 meals of roughly 25 to 40 g each is a practical way to get there.

A normal Indian vegetarian day of dal, roti and sabzi often lands around 50 to 60 g. Adding one dense source to each meal (curd at breakfast, paneer or soya at lunch, a dal plus tofu dinner) usually closes the gap without supplements.

3. Vegetables and fibre keep you full

ICMR-NIN's dietary guidelines suggest vegetables and fruits make up about half of what you eat in a day. Higher fibre intakes, roughly 25 to 29 g a day from whole foods, are linked in large studies to lower rates of heart disease, type 2 diabetes and bowel cancer. Practically: a sabzi or salad with lunch and dinner, a fruit a day, whole dals and some whole grains.

4. Carbs and fats are not the enemy

Once calories and protein are right, you can split the rest between carbs and fats in the way you enjoy. Keep fat to at least about 20 percent of calories for hormones and absorbing vitamins. Watch cooking oil: one tablespoon is about 120 kcal, and it is easy to pour three.

5. Go easy on added sugar and salt

ICMR-NIN advises keeping added sugar under 5 percent of your calories, and that includes jaggery and honey, which are still sugars. The WHO recommends less than 5 g of salt a day; packaged snacks, pickles and restaurant food are where most of it hides.

Build the plate

  • Half: vegetables or salad.
  • A quarter: a protein source such as dal, paneer, tofu, soya, curd or eggs.
  • A quarter: roti, rice or millets. Adjust this part up or down with your goal.
  • Add a bowl of curd or a glass of chaas if it fits your day.

How to check it is working

Weigh yourself a few mornings a week, after the toilet and before eating, and compare weekly averages rather than single days. If the average has not moved in the direction you want after 2 to 3 weeks, adjust by 100 to 200 kcal a day and give it another 2 weeks.
