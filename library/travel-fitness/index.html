Travel fitness

2 min read · By Pratik Jain

Work trips and holidays do not have to undo your progress, and they do not have to be perfect either. The aim is to keep the habit alive: move most days, eat protein at most meals, and sleep.

20-minute hotel-room workout

No equipment. Do each exercise for 40 seconds, rest 20 seconds, and go round 3 or 4 times.

  1. Bodyweight squat, or slow squats with a 3-second lowering
  2. Push-up, hands on the bed or desk to make it easier
  3. Reverse lunges, alternating legs
  4. Glute bridge, single-leg if it is too easy
  5. Plank

Got a hotel gym? Do your normal plan with whatever is there, or a simple full-body session: one squat or lunge, one press, one row, one hinge, plus core.

Walk the trip

Exploring on foot is training. Walk to meetings when it is safe and sensible, take the long way back to the hotel, and use the stairs.

Eating on the road

  • Hotel breakfast: eggs, idli or dosa with sambar, curd, fruit, and a coffee. Go easy on the pastries.
  • Airports and trains: carry roasted chana, nuts, protein bars and a whey or sattu sachet.
  • Client dinners: use the eating-out guide: protein first, one carb, drinks with water in between.
  • Holidays: enjoy the local food. Keep breakfast and one other meal fairly simple, and let the third be the experience.

Jet lag and sleep

Get daylight in the morning at your destination, keep caffeine early in the day, and try to sleep at local bedtime from the first night.

Coming back

Expect the scale to be up a little from salt, carbs and travel. It is mostly water and settles within a few days of normal routine. Go straight back to your plan; no detox needed.

General information, not medical advice. If you have a health condition, are pregnant or take medication, check with your doctor before changing your diet, training or supplements.
