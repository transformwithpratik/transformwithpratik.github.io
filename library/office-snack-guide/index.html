Healthy office snack guide

2 min read · By Pratik Jain

The 4 pm snack is where many good days go sideways: a biscuit packet, a samosa in a meeting, a sugary coffee. The fix is not willpower. It is having something better within arm's reach.

What makes a good work snack

  • Some protein (10 g or more) so it actually holds you until dinner.
  • Some fibre from fruit, chana, sprouts or nuts.
  • A sensible size: 150 to 250 kcal for most people.

Desk drawer (no fridge needed)

  • Roasted chana: a small bowl (30 g) gives about 6 g of protein.
  • Makhana and peanuts, roasted at home. See the recipe.
  • Peanuts or almonds, pre-portioned into small boxes so a handful stays a handful.
  • Whey protein or sattu sachets to mix with water.
  • Protein bars: read the label. Look for at least 15 g of protein and less sugar than protein.
  • Fruit that travels: bananas, apples, oranges.

Office fridge

  • Hung curd or Greek yogurt cups, with fruit.
  • Chana chaat or sprouts salad made the night before.
  • Paneer cubes with chaat masala and lemon.
  • Chaas or a glass of milk.

In the cafeteria

  • Pick a dal and a sabzi first, then add one roti or a small portion of rice.
  • Idli or dosa with sambar beats vada or puri; the sambar adds some protein.
  • Grilled or tandoori over fried and creamy gravies.
  • Eggetarian? An omelette or boiled eggs are among the easiest protein options at most counters.

Meetings and celebrations

Cake and samosas will appear. Have a piece if you want it, sit down, and enjoy it. One snack does not decide your week. What matters is that it is the exception, not the default.

Watch the drinks

Two sugary teas or coffees a day can add 150 to 250 kcal without you noticing. Try cutting the sugar by half for a week, then half again. And keep a water bottle on your desk; it is the simplest hydration habit there is.
