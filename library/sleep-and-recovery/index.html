Sleep and recovery

2 min read · By Pratik Jain

Sleep is where training turns into progress. Short or irregular sleep makes hunger harder to manage, workouts feel heavier, and motivation drops. None of the fixes below cost money.

How much

Sleep medicine groups recommend that adults get 7 or more hours a night on a regular basis. If you are often sleepy during the day, you probably need more than you are getting.

Five habits that help

1. Set a coffee cut-off

Across 24 studies, caffeine cut total sleep by about 45 minutes on average, sometimes without people noticing. For a regular cup of coffee, stopping roughly 9 hours before bed avoided most of the effect. Sleep at 11? Make 2 pm your last coffee. A regular cup of black tea had a much smaller effect in the same analysis.

2. Wake up at the same time, even on Sundays

In a large study of about 60,000 UK adults with wrist trackers, people with the most regular sleep had a lower risk of dying over the following years than the most irregular. It is an association in mostly older adults, not proof, but a fixed alarm is a free experiment.

3. Bright mornings, dim evenings

Light sets your body clock. Get outside in daylight in the morning, and dim the lights after dinner.

4. Phone out of bed

A trial of night-mode screen filters found no difference to sleep. What matters more is time in bed spent scrolling. Charge the phone outside the bedroom if you can.

5. Finish dinner 2 to 3 hours before bed

Lying down soon after a big meal is linked to more acid reflux. The evidence here is limited, but it is an easy habit, especially during wedding and festival season.

Recovery between workouts

  • Rest days are part of the plan. Walk on them; easy movement helps you feel less stiff.
  • Hit your protein and eat enough overall. Big deficits make recovery slower.
  • Lighter weeks. Every 6 to 8 weeks, or when life gets heavy, cut your training volume by about half for a week.
  • Soreness is not the goal. It fades as you adapt; progress is measured in reps and weight, not pain.

When to see a doctor

Loud snoring, waking up gasping, or being very sleepy during the day despite enough time in bed can be signs of sleep apnoea, which is common and treatable. Mouth taping is not a fix. Speak to a doctor.

  2. Gardiner C, et al. The effect of caffeine on subsequent sleep: a systematic review and meta-analysis. Sleep Med Rev. 2023;69:101764.
  3. Windred DP, et al. Sleep regularity is a stronger predictor of mortality risk than sleep duration: a prospective cohort study. Sleep. 2024;47(1).
  4. Duraccio KM, et al. Does iPhone Night Shift mitigate negative effects of smartphone use on sleep outcomes in emerging adults? Sleep Health. 2021;7(4):478-484.
