Office fitness

2 min read · By Pratik Jain

Long hours at a desk are the default for most professionals. You cannot always change the job, but you can change what happens around it.

Break up long sitting

Trials on breaking up sitting have mostly used short walking breaks about every 30 minutes. Practically: stand up when a call starts, walk to fill your water bottle, take the stairs to another floor's washroom, or set a gentle reminder for every 30 to 45 minutes.

Walk after meals

An easy 10-minute walk started within a few minutes of finishing a meal reliably blunts the rise in blood sugar after that meal. It is one of the best-supported small habits there is, and it pairs well with a post-lunch slump.

Desk-friendly moves

  • Calf raises while you wait for the kettle or the lift.
  • Hip flexor stretch once or twice a day to counter long sitting.
  • Stairs instead of the lift for one or two floors.
  • Walking one-on-ones and phone calls.

Standing desks

A standing desk cuts sitting time, but standing still is not exercise and long periods of standing come with their own aches. Use it as a cue to move, not as the goal.

Screens and eyes

  • People blink much less while looking at screens, and more blinks are only partial. Every so often, do a few slow, full blinks.
  • The 20-20-20 rule (every 20 minutes, look 20 feet away for 20 seconds) eased eye-strain symptoms in a small study, but only while people kept doing it.
  • Eye experts suggest a monitor slightly below eye level.
  • Blue-light glasses probably do not reduce eye strain, according to a Cochrane review. Save the money.

Food at work

Pack a protein-forward lunch, keep a water bottle on the desk, and have a planned snack for the afternoon. The office snack guide has a list.
